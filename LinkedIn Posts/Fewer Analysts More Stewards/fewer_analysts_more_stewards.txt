𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗯𝗼𝘁𝘁𝗹𝗲𝗻𝗲𝗰𝗸 𝘄𝗮𝘀 𝗻𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗱𝗮𝘀𝗵𝗯𝗼𝗮𝗿𝗱. 𝗜𝘁 𝘄𝗮𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗾𝘂𝗲𝘂𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗱 𝗶𝘁.
Hire analysts. Build dashboards. Wait for reports. That was the playbook at almost every company — and it worked, in the sense that it produced dashboards.

Whether it produced answers is a different question.

📉 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝘁𝗲𝗹𝗹: even as companies keep rolling out BI tools, actual usage across employees stays flat — around three in ten. That's not an adoption problem. It's a design problem. A dashboard answers the question someone thought to ask six weeks ago, at a grain someone else picked. The moment your question is slightly different, you're not looking at a dashboard. You're filing a ticket.

⚙️ 𝗔𝗻𝗱 𝗼𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂'𝗿𝗲 𝗳𝗶𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗸𝗲𝘁𝘀, 𝗵𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗰𝗼𝘂𝗻𝘁 𝗶𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘀𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗶𝗻𝘁. More questions means more analysts, more SQL, more backlog. Your data team's size ends up tracking your company's curiosity — not its revenue.

✍️ 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗼𝗹𝗲 𝗶𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝗱𝗶𝘀𝗮𝗽𝗽𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴. 𝗜𝘁'𝘀 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗻𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗴. Think translator vs. editor. The old model needed someone to translate every business question into SQL, one at a time, forever. The new model needs someone to get the definitions right once — what an "active customer" is, how refunds net against revenue, which timestamp is truth — so every future question gets answered consistently.

That's a steward, not an analyst. Smaller team. More senior. Spending its time on the questions that actually need a human.

𝗕𝘂𝘁 𝗵𝗲𝗿𝗲'𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗰𝗵.
An agent is only as trustworthy as what it's standing on. Point one at disconnected spreadsheets and three "sources of truth" that disagree, and you haven't removed the bottleneck — you've just made the wrong answers arrive faster and more confidently.

Skip the foundation and you're not modernizing your data team. You're automating the confusion.
