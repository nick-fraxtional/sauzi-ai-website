The Trust-Speed Paradox: AI Made Your Data Team Faster. Did It Make Them Right?

72% of data teams now use AI to write code. Only 24% check what it produces. That gap is the quiet risk in every "we move faster now" story.

72% of data teams now use AI to write their code. Only 24% invest in checking what it produces.

Those two numbers come from dbt's latest research, and the distance between them is the quiet risk hiding in every "we move faster with AI now" story. Speed went up. Verification didn't. The team got a force multiplier on output and kept the same — or less — investment in confirming that output is right.

How the Gap Actually Bites

It rarely looks like a disaster. It looks like a Tuesday.

An agent writes a transformation in seconds. The query runs without error. A number lands on a dashboard. Someone makes a decision on that number — adjusts spend, reforecasts inventory, reports up. Nobody stopped to ask whether the underlying logic was correct, because nothing prompted them to. It looked right. It ran. Green checkmark.

The failure isn't that the AI writes bad code. It's that AI-written code fails silently. A join that quietly double-counts. A filter that drops refunds. A definition of "active customer" that doesn't match the one finance uses. The output is plausible, the pipeline is green, and the error only surfaces weeks later when two teams show up to a meeting with numbers that don't agree.

The Answer Isn't to Slow Down

The instinct is to add a human gate — make someone eyeball every AI-generated model before it ships. That doesn't scale, and it throws away the speed you just gained.

The better move is to make "correct" something the system checks for you, automatically, every time:

  • Tests on the data, not just the code. Assertions on uniqueness, referential integrity, accepted values, and row counts catch the silent failures a passing query won't.
  • Definitions that live in one place. When "active customer" or "net revenue" is defined once in a transformation layer, the AI inherits the right meaning instead of inventing its own.
  • A documented path everything flows through. If every AI-written transformation has to pass through a layer that's tested and reviewed by design, verification stops being a manual favor and becomes part of the pipeline.

Fast and trustworthy aren't opposites. You have to build for both — and most teams have only built for fast.

Why We Don't Do Black Boxes

This is the practical reason behind a principle we hold to: no black boxes. If you can't see what the AI did — which tables it touched, what logic it wrote, what assumptions it made — you can't know whether it was right. You're just trusting a confident output. Every pipeline, model, and definition we build is documented and inspectable, because that's the only version of "fast" that's also safe to bet a decision on.

AI accelerating your data work is real, and it's worth having. But acceleration without verification isn't productivity. It's risk you haven't noticed yet.

If an AI wrote half your pipeline this quarter, who reviewed it?
